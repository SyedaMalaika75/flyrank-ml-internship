Ranking Search-Visibility Decline Signals for Content Review Prioritization

Syeda Malaika - FlyRank Machine Learning Capstone

Abstract

This capstone asks whether public-safe page-level content and search-performance signals can help rank pages associated with observed search-visibility decline. A leakage-safe Logistic Regression model was evaluated with a client-grouped holdout split and compared with a simple rule baseline on the same held-out pages. Logistic Regression improved Precision@20 from 0.40 to 0.70 and Precision@50 from 0.42 to 0.72. The result is directional decision-support for prioritizing manual content review, not a guarantee of future decline or proof that a refresh causes ranking improvement.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams often have more pages than they can review at once. This work asks which pages should be reviewed first and whether a learned ranking can improve that prioritization over transparent rules.

2. Data

The unit of analysis is one anonymized content page. The evaluation uses 23,837 training rows and 6,163 testing rows with an 80/20 split grouped by client. The model uses 32 public-safe content metadata and trailing 90-day performance features. Client names, domains, URLs and private queries are excluded.

3. Methodology

Logistic Regression is used as a ranking model. The target marks observed downward visibility movement. Outcome-derived fields such as trend_direction and trend_pct, identifiers, and recent trend-window fields are excluded. The rule baseline prioritizes stale visible pages and visible low-CTR pages, with impressions as a tie-breaker. Both approaches use the same held-out clients.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The model measures associations, not causation.
  • A high score does not guarantee future decline.
  • Some decline cases are ranked below the top 50.
  • Results are specific to this anonymized dataset and evaluation design.
  • Human review should remain part of the decision process.

6. Ranked Recommendations

  1. Review the highest-scored pages first.
  2. Prioritize pages with meaningful visibility and enough evidence for review.
  3. Use stale-content and low-CTR signals as transparent reasons for investigation.
  4. Require human confirmation before changing content.
  5. Monitor outcomes after any refresh or intervention.

9. Conclusion

On the client-grouped held-out evaluation, Logistic Regression produced a more precise ranking than the rule baseline. The practical conclusion is limited but useful: the learned score can improve the order in which a team reviews pages associated with observed decline patterns, as directional decision-support rather than an automatic action.
